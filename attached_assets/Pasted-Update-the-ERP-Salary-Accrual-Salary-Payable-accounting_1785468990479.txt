Update the ERP Salary Accrual / Salary Payable accounting logic.

IMPORTANT:
This is an accounting correction involving Attendance → Payroll → Salary Expense →
Salary Payable → Dashboard/Reports.

Do NOT make this a frontend-only change.

First inspect the existing attendance, payroll, salary accrual, salary payable,
journal posting, Chart of Accounts, P&L, dashboard payable, and salary payment logic.

Do not publish automatically.
Implement and test in development first.

==================================================
1. CURRENT PROBLEM
==================================================

Currently salary is being accrued daily using the employee's fixed monthly salary.

Example:

Monthly salary = ₹30,000

The ERP may currently calculate a fixed daily amount and automatically post it every
day to:

Dr Salary Expense
Cr Salary Payable

This is NOT the required behaviour.

An employee should not create a full day's salary expense simply because that
calendar/work day exists.

Actual salary earned depends on attendance/payroll information such as:

- Present
- Full Day
- Half Day
- Absent
- LOP
- Approved paid leave where applicable
- Overtime where applicable
- Earnings
- Deductions
- Other existing payroll rules

Therefore accounting must follow the employee's ACTUAL EARNED / NET PAYABLE salary.

==================================================
2. AUTHORITATIVE SALARY AMOUNT
==================================================

Use the existing payroll engine as the source of truth.

Attendance / payroll calculation
        ↓
Actual salary earned
        ↓
Applicable earnings/deductions
        ↓
NET PAYABLE
        ↓
Accounting

Do NOT create a second independent salary calculation inside the accounting module.

The same amount that payroll considers actually payable to the employee must drive
Salary Expense / Salary Payable according to the ERP's payroll accounting policy.

==================================================
3. ATTENDANCE-BASED ACCRUAL
==================================================

As attendance becomes known, salary accrual must reflect the employee's actual
earned salary.

Example conceptually:

Employee monthly salary = ₹30,000.

If today's attendance results in a full paid day:
→ accrue the applicable full-day earned salary.

If today's attendance results in a half day:
→ accrue only the applicable half-day earned salary.

If today's attendance results in unpaid absence / LOP:
→ do not accrue a full day's salary.

If attendance/payroll is subsequently corrected:
→ the salary accrual must adjust accordingly.

Do not leave the original incorrect accrual in the books and then add another full
entry on top.

==================================================
4. NET PAYABLE
==================================================

The key amount I need is the employee's NET PAYABLE salary.

When payroll calculations include applicable deductions/adjustments, the accounting
payable must ultimately agree with the amount actually owed to the employee.

Example:

Calculated earned salary       ₹20,000
Applicable deductions           ₹2,000
---------------------------------------
Net Payable                     ₹18,000

The Salary Payable representing the employee amount due must ultimately be ₹18,000,
not blindly ₹30,000.

IMPORTANT:
Before implementing, inspect how the existing ERP accounts for deductions.

If some deductions require separate liability ledgers such as statutory payable
accounts, preserve proper double-entry accounting.

Do NOT simply debit Salary Expense by "Net Pay" if that would incorrectly omit an
expense or liability that must be separately recognized.

The required result is:

- Salary Expense must represent the correct payroll expense according to the payroll
  calculation.
- Salary Payable must represent the actual amount payable to the employee.
- Separate deduction/payable ledgers must remain correct where applicable.
- All entries must balance.

==================================================
5. DAILY PROFITABILITY
==================================================

I still need daily profitability to be accurate.

But daily salary expense must be based on salary actually earned from attendance /
payroll status, NOT a blind fixed monthly salary accrual.

After attendance is finalized/updated for the day, the accounting books should
reflect the appropriate earned salary.

This should allow:

Sales
- Purchases
- Other Expenses
- Actual earned Salary Expense

to produce a meaningful daily P&L.

==================================================
6. CHECK-IN / CHECK-OUT
==================================================

Use the ERP's existing attendance rules.

Check-in / Check-out
        ↓
Attendance result
        ↓
Full Day / Half Day / Absent / etc.
        ↓
Payroll earned amount
        ↓
Net Payable / payroll accounting

Do NOT invent new attendance thresholds if they already exist.

Reuse the existing attendance/payroll engine.

If attendance has not yet been finalized for the day, follow the ERP's existing
attendance status rules rather than assuming a full paid day.

==================================================
7. ATTENDANCE CORRECTIONS
==================================================

This is critical.

Suppose the ERP initially records:

Full Day

and later an authorized user corrects it to:

Half Day.

The salary accounting for that period must adjust to the new payroll result.

Likewise:

Absent → Present
Half Day → Full Day
Full Day → LOP
etc.

must produce the correct accounting adjustment.

Use safe delta/reversal/recalculation logic.

Never duplicate the original salary expense.

==================================================
8. MONTH-END PAYROLL
==================================================

At month end, payroll must reconcile exactly with accounting.

For each employee:

Final payroll Net Payable
↔ employee Salary Payable balance attributable to that payroll

The monthly payroll approval process should validate/finalize the accrued amounts.

Approval must NOT create the entire salary expense again if the expense/payable has
already been accrued through the attendance/payroll process.

No duplicate salary expense.

==================================================
9. SALARY PAYMENT
==================================================

Salary payment remains separate from salary accrual.

When salary is actually paid:

Dr Salary Payable
Cr Cash / Bank

for the amount being paid, subject to existing payroll/payment rules.

Payment must NOT create Salary Expense again.

If partially paid:

Salary Payable should retain only the remaining unpaid balance.

Example:

Salary Payable = ₹18,000
Paid = ₹10,000

Remaining Salary Payable = ₹8,000.

==================================================
10. CHART OF ACCOUNTS
==================================================

The accounting hierarchy must remain clear.

Indirect Expenses
    → Salary
        → applicable salary expense ledgers

Current Liabilities
    → Salary Payable
        → employee salary payable ledgers where the existing architecture uses them

Example:

Salary Payable
    → Ramis
    → Employee B
    → Employee C

Do not create duplicate employee salary ledgers if they already exist.

==================================================
11. DASHBOARD PAYABLE
==================================================

Salary Payable must be included correctly in the ERP's total current payable
figures/dashboard where liabilities are represented.

If accounting currently shows:

Vendor Payable          ₹50,000
Salary Payable          ₹18,000
Rent Payable            ₹20,000

then the relevant total payable/current-liability reporting should reconcile to the
underlying ledgers.

Do not maintain a separate dashboard salary number that disagrees with Chart of
Accounts.

Use the authoritative accounting balances.

==================================================
12. PROFIT & LOSS
==================================================

P&L must use the corrected Salary Expense.

It must NOT show fixed salary expense for days where the employee did not actually
earn that amount.

The monthly P&L salary figure must reconcile to payroll expense.

The daily P&L should reflect earned salary based on available/finalized attendance.

==================================================
13. BALANCE SHEET
==================================================

Salary Payable must appear correctly under Current Liabilities.

Balance Sheet Salary Payable
↔ Chart of Accounts Salary Payable

must agree.

Once salary is paid, the liability must decrease accordingly.

==================================================
14. GENERAL LEDGER / TRIAL BALANCE
==================================================

Verify the corrected salary postings flow through:

- General Ledger
- Trial Balance
- Profit & Loss
- Balance Sheet
- Chart of Accounts
- Dashboard
- Payroll reports
- Employee salary payable

There must not be different versions of Salary Payable in different modules.

==================================================
15. DO NOT BREAK MANUAL ADJUSTMENTS
==================================================

If an authorized Journal/Payment/other valid accounting voucher adjusts an employee's
Salary Payable ledger, the current accounting balance displayed elsewhere must
respect that ledger movement.

Do not calculate current Salary Payable solely as:

Payroll generated - payroll payment records

if that ignores valid accounting journal movements.

Current accounting balance must reconcile with the ledger.

Keep payroll history and accounting balance conceptually separate where necessary.

- payroll calculation

Find the actual source of the current fixed daily salary accrual and replace/correct

==================================================
17. IDEMPOTENCY
==================================================

The accrual process must be idempotent.

Running payroll/accrual calculation twice must NOT double:

- Salary Expense
- Salary Payable
- deduction liabilities
- employee payable

Use deterministic payroll/accrual references or the existing safe architecture.

==================================================
19. EXISTING DATA SAFETY
==================================================

Do NOT automatically rewrite historical production payroll.

Do NOT create balancing journal entries.

Do NOT delete payroll records.

Do NOT reset attendance.

Do NOT alter production employee salary data during testing.

If existing historical salary accruals are inconsistent with the new logic, report
the affected records/periods and proposed correction separately before changing
financial history.

==================================================
20. FINAL REQUIREMENT
==================================================

The fundamental rule is:

DO NOT ACCRUE SALARY SIMPLY BECAUSE THE EMPLOYEE HAS A FIXED MONTHLY SALARY.

Instead:

Attendance
→ payroll calculation
→ actual earned salary
→ deductions/adjustments
→ employee Net Payable
→ Salary Expense / Salary Payable accounting

The amount actually owed to the employee must be reflected in Salary Payable.

Salary accounting must then reconcile across:

Payroll
Chart of Accounts
P&L
Balance Sheet
Trial Balance
General Ledger
Dashboard Payables

No duplicate expense and no duplicate payable.
