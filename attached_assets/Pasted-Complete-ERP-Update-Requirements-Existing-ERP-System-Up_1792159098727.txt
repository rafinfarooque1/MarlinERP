Complete ERP Update Requirements – Existing ERP System

1. Dashboard and Reports – Export to Excel

* Add an Export to Excel button next to the existing Share button on every dashboard and report where a Share button is available.
* The exported Excel file must contain the relevant data displayed in the dashboard or report, respecting the selected date range, global filters, location filters, and user permissions.
* Ensure that the Excel export works correctly across all applicable modules and reports.

2. Point of Sale (POS)

* Add a Salesman column to the Point of Sale list.
* Display the name of the salesman assigned to each sale.
* Add an Item-wise checkbox or button at the bottom-right corner of the POS screen.
* When Item-wise is selected, display the individual items included in each sale, allowing users to view sales item by item.
* Ensure that the existing POS workflow, sales records, payment collection, and reports continue to work correctly.

3. Cash Balance and Bank Deposits

* In the Cash Balance module, retain and display the history of deposits made to the bank.
* Remove the Mark as Received option from the bank deposit workflow.
* When a deposit is recorded as deposited to the bank, automatically create an entry in the Bank Reconciliation module.
* The deposit must appear in the reconciliation list and should affect the appropriate bank ledger only after reconciliation.
* Maintain a complete deposit history for reference and auditing.

4. Receipt Vouchers and Payment Vouchers

Receipt Vouchers

* Sales vouchers must never appear in the Receipt Vouchers list. Remove any existing logic that incorrectly displays sales vouchers as receipt vouchers.
* The Receipt Vouchers list must contain only:
    1. Receipt vouchers entered manually.
    2. Receipts recorded through the POS Collect Payment workflow when the customer pays through a bank, UPI, or another supported electronic payment method.
* These receipts must be recorded correctly and sent to the Bank Reconciliation module when applicable.
* Do not create duplicate receipt entries for the same transaction.
* Keep sales vouchers and receipt vouchers as separate transaction types.

Payment Vouchers

* The Payment Vouchers list must contain only actual payment vouchers entered through the manual payment voucher workflow.
* Do not display purchase vouchers, unrelated transactions, or other voucher types as payment vouchers.
* Maintain proper separation between payment vouchers, receipt vouchers, sales vouchers, and purchase vouchers.
* Electronic payments and applicable bank transactions must be available for reconciliation without creating duplicate accounting entries.

5. Live Stock – Storage Location and Item Tracking

* Combine the Storage Location, Item Tracking, and related stock information into a single, convenient interface.
* Display the Storage Location directly in the Live Stock list.
* Provide an option to edit the storage location directly from the Live Stock screen, subject to the user’s permissions.
* Add an information (i) button for each stock item.
* When the user clicks the information button, display the complete item tracking history, including the relevant stock movements, transaction details, and storage location information.
* Users should be able to view the storage location and item tracking details without navigating to separate pages.
* Improve the existing UI so the information is presented clearly and efficiently on one screen.

6. Production, Customers, Purchases, Vendors, and Existing Modules

* The existing Production batches module and Production reports are already available. Preserve their functionality.

7. Inventory Verification, Stock Loss, Damage, Wastage, and Expiry

Create a proper accounting workflow for stock verification and inventory adjustments.

* When stock verification is performed and the actual stock quantity is lower or higher than the recorded quantity, record the difference correctly.
* When stock is damaged, wasted, or expired in any warehouse, record the relevant inventory adjustment.
* Create or configure a dedicated ledger named Stock Verification and map it correctly within the Chart of Accounts.
* All stock verification adjustments must create appropriate ledger entries.
* Stock shortages, damage, wastage, and expiry must be reflected as losses in the Profit and Loss (P&L) statement.
* Stock shortages and losses should reduce the P&L appropriately.
* When verified stock is higher than the recorded stock, account for the adjustment using the correct accounting treatment rather than automatically treating every adjustment as a loss.
* Ensure that stock quantities, inventory valuation, the Stock Verification ledger, the General Ledger, and the P&L remain consistent.
* Record the affected warehouse, item, quantity, reason for adjustment, date, and value so that the adjustment can be traced and audited.
* Avoid duplicate postings or incorrect automatic ledger creation.

8. Assets Module

9. HR – Employee Attendance Permissions

Implement warehouse-specific attendance permissions.

* If an employee has the relevant attendance permission enabled, that employee must be able to enter check-in and check-out records for employees belonging to the warehouse assigned to them.
* This functionality must be available to authorized employees, not only administrators.
* The permission must allow check-in and check-out entry only. It must not automatically grant permission to edit employee profiles, approve payroll, access other HR information, or perform unrelated administrative actions.
* Employees must only be able to record attendance for their assigned warehouse.
* They must not be able to view or modify attendance for other warehouses unless explicitly authorized by a separate permission.
* Enforce these restrictions in both the user interface and the backend/API.
* Ensure that attendance records are saved against the correct employee, warehouse, date, and time.

10. Payroll and Employee Salary Downloads

11. Warehouse Disable/Delete Behaviour

* Ensure that disabling a warehouse does not corrupt historical financial data or break existing reports.

* Apply the selected location filter consistently across:
    * All dashboards.
    * All reports.
    * Point of Sale.
    * Live Stock and inventory.
    * Stock transfers.
    * Purchases and vendors, wherever location filtering applies.
    * Sales and customers, wherever location filtering applies.

    * HR and attendance reports.

* Do not rely only on frontend filtering. Enforce the location restrictions in backend queries and APIs so unselected location data cannot be retrieved through direct requests.
* Ensure that changing the Global Filter immediately updates the displayed data and totals.
* Verify that no stale, cached, or previously selected location data remains visible after changing the filter.
* The Admin must see only the data permitted by the currently selected locations and applicable access rules.
* Preserve legitimate consolidated reporting when all relevant locations are intentionally selected or when an authorized report explicitly requires consolidated data.

14. Accounting Reports and Bank Reconciliation – Rebuild the Workflow

The existing Bank Reconciliation workflow must be reviewed and corrected comprehensively. Remove or replace the incorrect reconciliation logic and rebuild the workflow according to the requirements below.

14.1 Start Reconciliation Fresh

* Review the existing reconciliation records, bank ledger entries, electronic payment records, and accounting postings before changing the implementation.
* Reset or clear the existing reconciliation list as required so reconciliation can start from the beginning.
* Do not blindly delete historical accounting transactions or posted ledger entries. Reverse or reset existing reconciliation-related postings safely, maintain an audit trail, and prevent duplicate entries.
* Ensure that transactions previously entered through POS, Receipt Vouchers, Payment Vouchers, electronic payments, or direct bank entries are correctly available for reconciliation.
* Make sure the new workflow does not duplicate existing transactions.

14.2 Transactions That Must Appear in Reconciliation

The reconciliation list must include all applicable unreconciled bank and electronic payment transactions, including:

* POS payments collected through bank or UPI.
* Electronic payments.
* UPI transactions.
* Online payments.
* Applicable manually entered Receipt Vouchers.
* Applicable manually entered Payment Vouchers.
* Bank deposits recorded through Cash Balance.
* Applicable direct bank transactions, including transactions recorded against a bank such as Federal Bank.
* Other supported bank-related transactions that have not yet been reconciled.

All applicable transactions must flow directly into the reconciliation list without requiring unnecessary intermediate cash book assignments.

Transactions must not be considered reconciled merely because they were entered in POS, Receipt Vouchers, Payment Vouchers, or a bank account. They must remain pending until reconciliation is completed.

14.3 Bank Ledger Posting Rules

* Transactions must not affect the final bank ledger balance through the reconciliation workflow until they are reconciled.
* Once a transaction is reconciled, post the appropriate accounting entry to the selected bank ledger.
* If reconciliation is reset, reverse the reconciliation-generated posting safely and return the transaction to the pending reconciliation list.
* Do not remove legitimate original transaction entries when resetting reconciliation.
* Ensure that the bank ledger balance, reconciliation records, and General Ledger remain consistent.
* Prevent duplicate bank ledger postings if the same transaction is reconciled, reset, and reconciled again.

14.4 Reconciliation List – Simple Inline Interface

The reconciliation screen must be simple and easy to use. Do not create separate pop-up windows for normal reconciliation.

The list must display the relevant transaction information, including:

* Transaction date.
* Bill number or transaction reference.
* Transaction amount.
* Warehouse/location.
* Payment method or transaction type.
* Reconciliation status.

For each transaction, provide the required reconciliation controls directly within the list or the same screen.

The user must be able to select the bank, processing charge, and reconciliation date without leaving the reconciliation screen.

14.5 Single-Transaction Reconciliation

For a single transaction:

1. Select the unreconciled transaction from the list.
2. Select the appropriate bank ledger.
3. Enter or select the processing charge, if applicable.
4. Select the date on which the entry should be posted to the bank ledger.
5. Click Reconcile.

The system must calculate the final amount correctly, post the entry to the selected bank ledger using the selected reconciliation date, and update the reconciliation status.

14.6 Multiple-Transaction Reconciliation

* Allow multiple unreconciled transactions to be selected from the same list.
* When multiple transactions are selected, provide one bank selection, one reconciliation date, and the applicable processing charge input for the selected group.
* Reconcile all selected transactions together through a single action.
* The system must calculate the total of all selected transactions and apply the processing charge correctly.
* When multiple transactions are reconciled together, the bank ledger posting should be consolidated into one appropriate net entry for that bank, with sufficient references or linked transaction details to maintain traceability.
* The user should not have to open separate pop-up windows for individual transactions.
* If selected transactions belong to different banks or incompatible transaction groups, prevent incorrect consolidation and ask the user to separate the selections as necessary.
* Maintain the original transaction-level reconciliation details even when the bank ledger posting is consolidated.

14.7 Processing Charge Calculation

Processing charges must be calculated consistently.

* If the processing charge is negative, add its absolute amount to the selected transaction total.
* If the processing charge is positive, subtract it from the selected transaction total.
* If there is no processing charge, use the original selected transaction total.
* Display the original total, processing charge, and final reconciled amount clearly before reconciliation.
* Post the correct net amount to the selected bank ledger.
* Record the processing charge in the appropriate accounting ledger or expense account according to the existing accounting structure.
* Do not treat the processing charge merely as a number added to or subtracted from the bank balance without recording the corresponding accounting entry.
* Ensure that the processing charge treatment is consistent for both single and multiple reconciliation.

14.8 Bank and UPI Selection

* Provide a Bank selection option when reconciling a bank transaction.
* For UPI and online transactions, display the appropriate payment method and relevant bank or settlement details.
* Allow the user to choose the appropriate bank ledger for reconciliation.
* Support all relevant bank accounts, not only the bank accounts currently associated with a specific cash book.
* Reconciliation must not be restricted to a single bank or a particular cash book.
* The selected bank ledger must receive the correct reconciled entry.

14.9 Reconciliation Ledger Details

* After reconciliation, the entry must appear in the appropriate bank ledger with the selected reconciliation date.
* Add or improve an information (i) button in the bank ledger so the user can inspect the details of the reconciled entry.
* The information view must show the reconciled amount, related transactions, original amounts, processing charge, final net amount, date, and relevant references.
* For consolidated reconciliation entries, allow the user to identify all the original transactions included in the total.
* Preserve an audit trail of the reconciliation action and any subsequent reversal or reset.

14.10 Remove the Cash Book/Bank Book Reconciliation Toggle

* Remove the unnecessary reconciliation toggle button from Cash Book and Bank Book.
* Reconciliation must be managed through the dedicated Bank Reconciliation module.
* Keep Cash Book and Bank Book focused on their respective accounting records and balances.
* If the existing Cash Book or Bank Book modules are no longer required in their current form, review dependencies before removing them. Do not break valid accounting functionality.

15. Chart of Accounts, General Ledger, and P&L Integration

The Chart of Accounts and General Ledger must be properly integrated with every relevant ERP module.

15.1 Every Relevant Transaction Must Be Accounted For

* All relevant transactions created in the ERP must generate the appropriate ledger entries.
* This includes sales, purchases, receipt vouchers, payment vouchers, reconciliation, stock transfers, stock verification, inventory adjustments, attendance/payroll-related accounting, and other modules that have financial effects.
* Not every operational event must create a financial entry. Only events with an accounting impact should post to the General Ledger, according to the applicable accounting rules.
* Ensure that every transaction with a financial impact is mapped to the correct ledger account.
* Every ledger entry must be traceable to its originating transaction.
* The Chart of Accounts and P&L must reflect the correct accounting entries.
* Prevent duplicate postings, missing ledgers, and incorrect automatic ledger creation.

15.2 Chart of Accounts Integration

15.3 Remove Unnecessary or Incorrect System-Generated Ledgers

15.4 Correct Inter-Branch and Warehouse Ledger Names

* Where separate inter-branch ledgers are required, use clear and consistent names based on the actual source and destination locations.
* Ensure that warehouse names are displayed correctly in the Chart of Accounts, General Ledger, reconciliation details, and reports.
* Do not create unnecessary duplicate ledgers for every warehouse combination if the existing accounting design can correctly handle the transactions through properly mapped accounts and references.

15.5 P&L Accuracy

* Ensure that stock verification losses, damage, wastage, and expiry are reflected in the P&L using the correct expense or loss accounts.
* Ensure that reconciliation processing charges are recorded correctly.
* Ensure that sales, purchases, expenses, payroll-related accounting, and other financial transactions are reflected correctly.
