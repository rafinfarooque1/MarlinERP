FINAL CORE CONTINUITY HARDENING
OPENING BALANCE = PREVIOUS CLOSING BALANCE
OPENING STOCK = PREVIOUS CLOSING STOCK
===============================================================

This is a critical accounting and inventory continuity requirement.

Do NOT publish production.
Do NOT modify production data.
Do NOT create production transactions.

We need to guarantee that daily and period reporting is mathematically
continuous.

CORE RULE:

For every location, account, customer/vendor ledger where applicable,
and inventory item:

                TODAY CLOSING
                     =
             TOMORROW OPENING

For inventory:

                TODAY CLOSING STOCK
                     =
             TOMORROW OPENING STOCK

There must never be a gap, overlap, or recalculation drift between
consecutive business dates.


===============================================================
1. BUSINESS DATE MUST BE THE AUTHORITATIVE DATE
===============================================================

Use the ERP/company business timezone already configured.

Do NOT use server UTC date directly for daily accounting/reporting.

All opening/closing calculations must use the same business-date logic.

Example:

16/09/2026 closing
        ↓
17/09/2026 opening

The transition must happen according to the company's business date,
not server UTC midnight.


===============================================================
2. CASH BALANCE CONTINUITY
===============================================================

For every Cash account and location:

Opening Balance for date D
=
Closing Balance for date D-1

Example:

15 Sep Closing Cash = ₹73,221.20

16 Sep Opening Cash MUST be:

₹73,221.20

If there are no transactions on 16 Sep:

16 Sep Closing Cash MUST also be:

₹73,221.20

Do not recompute the opening balance from an unrelated current balance.

Opening balance must be derived from the previous authoritative closing
position.


===============================================================
3. BANK BALANCE CONTINUITY
===============================================================

For every bank account and location:

Opening Bank Balance for date D
=
Closing Bank Balance for date D-1

Example:

15 Sep Closing Bank = ₹22,393.16

16 Sep Opening Bank = ₹22,393.16

If no transactions occur:

16 Sep Closing Bank = ₹22,393.16


Do not use:

- current bank balance
- today's balance
- reconciliation status
- current account master balance

as a substitute for the historical opening balance.


===============================================================
4. CUSTOMER LEDGER CONTINUITY
===============================================================

For every customer and location:

Opening Customer Balance on date D
=
Closing Customer Balance on date D-1

Example:

15 Sep closing customer receivable = ₹10,000

16 Sep opening customer receivable = ₹10,000

Then transactions on 16 Sep produce the new closing balance.

Do not double-count receipts, bill allocations, credit notes, advances,
or sales.

Remember the existing receipt architecture:

Financial Receipt
=
ONE financial transaction

Bill allocations
=
settlement metadata

Bill allocations must NOT independently alter opening/closing ledger
balances.


===============================================================
5. VENDOR LEDGER CONTINUITY
===============================================================

For every vendor and location:

Opening Vendor Payable on date D
=
Closing Vendor Payable on date D-1

Purchases, purchase returns, payments, advances and adjustments occurring
on date D change the closing balance.

Do not recalculate the opening balance from today's vendor master data.


===============================================================
6. GENERAL LEDGER CONTINUITY
===============================================================

For every ledger account:

Opening Balance(D)
=
Closing Balance(D-1)

This must work for:

- Cash
- Bank
- Customers
- Vendors
- Sales
- Purchases
- Inventory
- COGS
- Expenses
- Income
- Fixed Assets
- Accumulated Depreciation
- Equity
- Other accounting accounts

Do not allow different reports to calculate opening balances using
different formulas.


===============================================================
7. INVENTORY OPENING STOCK
===============================================================

This is especially important.

For every:

Location
+
Item
+
Batch where applicable

the rule is:

Opening Stock(D)
=
Closing Stock(D-1)

Example:

15 Sep:

Opening Qty = 100 kg
Stock In = 20 kg
Stock Out = 30 kg
Closing Qty = 90 kg

Then:

16 Sep Opening Qty MUST = 90 kg


If there are no transactions on 16 Sep:

16 Sep Closing Qty MUST = 90 kg.


===============================================================
8. INVENTORY VALUE CONTINUITY
===============================================================

Quantity continuity alone is not enough.

For inventory valuation:

Closing inventory value(D-1)
must become
Opening inventory value(D)

using the authoritative historical stock-cost checkpoint.

Do NOT use today's mutable item master average cost to reconstruct
historical opening stock.

Use the existing:

stock_cost_snapshots

and authoritative stock movement valuation architecture.

The existing precise location cost checkpoint must remain authoritative.


===============================================================
9. BATCH / FIFO / FEFO CONTINUITY
===============================================================

For batch-controlled inventory:

Closing batches on date D
=
Opening batches on date D+1

Preserve:

- batch number
- expiry date
- quantity
- cost
- location

FIFO/FEFO layers must not disappear or be recreated differently simply
because the reporting date changed.

A historical report must not use today's batch state as historical state.


===============================================================
10. STOCK TRANSFER CONTINUITY
===============================================================

Internal stock transfers must maintain continuity.

Example:

Warehouse A:

15 Sep closing:
100 kg

Transfer out:
20 kg

Warehouse B receives:
20 kg

Result:

Warehouse A closing:
80 kg

Warehouse B closing:
120 kg

Next day:

Warehouse A opening:
80 kg

Warehouse B opening:
120 kg


Transfers must remain P&L neutral.

Do not create artificial:

- Sales
- Purchases
- Revenue
- Profit
- COGS

for same-GST internal inventory movements.

Different-GST statutory handling must still follow the existing ERP rules,
but company-level inventory continuity and profitability must remain
correct.


===============================================================
11. IN-TRANSIT STOCK
===============================================================

Be extremely careful with in-transit inventory.

If stock is dispatched on date D but received on date D+1:

At D closing:

Source:
decreased appropriately

Destination:
stock remains in transit until receipt according to existing model

At D+1 when received:

Destination:
receiving transaction increases stock

The historical opening/closing values must reflect the actual transfer
lifecycle.

Do not allow current transfer status to contaminate historical reports.

===============================================================

===============================================================

All Locations opening/closing values must equal the sum of the

requires additive consolidation.

Example:

Warehouse A closing stock = ₹100,000
Warehouse B closing stock = ₹200,000

All Locations closing stock:

₹300,000

Next day All Locations opening stock:

₹300,000

Do not use a separate independently calculated value that can drift from
the location totals.

===============================================================
22. BACKDATED TRANSACTION HANDLING
===============================================================

This is critical.

If a transaction is entered later with an earlier business date:

Example:

15 Sep closing was already calculated.

On 17 Sep a valid transaction dated 15 Sep is entered.

The system must either:

A. Rebuild/recalculate affected historical closing checkpoints and all
subsequent opening balances safely,

OR

B. Prevent the backdated mutation after the relevant period is locked.

Do NOT allow:

15 Sep closing = ₹100,000
16 Sep opening = ₹100,000

while a backdated 15 Sep transaction causes the true closing to become
₹90,000.

The continuity chain must remain intact.
