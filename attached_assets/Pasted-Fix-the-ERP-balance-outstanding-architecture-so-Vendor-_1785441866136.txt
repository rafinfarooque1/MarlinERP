==================================================
2. VENDOR PAYABLES
==================================================

Vendor payable must be based on the vendor's accounting ledger balance.

Example:

Purchase from Frozen Hub:

Dr Purchase / Inventory     ₹6,33,194
Cr Frozen Hub               ₹6,33,194

Vendor payable = ₹6,33,194.

If subsequently a valid JOURNAL posts:

Dr Frozen Hub               ₹6,33,194
Cr Frozen Hub Investment    ₹6,33,194

then:

Frozen Hub ledger = ₹0
Vendor payable = ₹0
Vendor list Balance = ₹0
Vendor detail Outstanding = ₹0
Payables report = ₹0
Dashboard Accounts Payable contribution = ₹0

The ERP must NOT continue showing ₹6,33,194 merely because no record exists in a
vendor_payments table.

The same must work when the vendor ledger is affected by:

- Purchase
- Payment voucher
- Journal voucher
- Contra, if the accounting rules legitimately allow that ledger
- Debit note / purchase return
- Opening balance
- Other valid posted accounting transactions

Do NOT automatically create a fake vendor-payment record merely to make the report
agree.

The accounting balance should naturally reconcile from the posted ledger entries.

==================================================
3. CUSTOMER RECEIVABLES
==================================================

Apply the same principle to customers.

Example:

Sale:

Dr Customer       ₹10,000
Cr Sales          ₹10,000

Receivable = ₹10,000.

If Receipt:

Dr Bank/Cash      ₹10,000
Cr Customer       ₹10,000

Customer ledger = ₹0
Receivable = ₹0.

But ALSO if an authorized Journal legitimately posts:

Dr Adjustment/Other Ledger
Cr Customer       ₹10,000

and the customer ledger becomes zero, then the customer current receivable must also
be zero.

Customer list, Customer detail, Outstanding, Receivables report and Dashboard must
agree with the ledger.

Do not make sale_payments the only mechanism capable of reducing current
receivables.

==================================================
4. CASH BALANCE
==================================================

Cash balance must also be ledger-driven.

Every valid posting affecting a Cash ledger must immediately affect:

- Cash & Bank screen
- Cash Book
- Dashboard Cash Balance
- relevant financial reports
- Trial Balance

This includes:

Payment:
Cr Cash

Receipt:
Dr Cash

Contra:
Dr/Cr Cash as appropriate

Journal:
Dr/Cr Cash if the voucher rules permit the posting

Expense:
Cr Cash where applicable

Sale/Purchase:
according to their actual accounting posting.

Do not maintain a separate cash total that ignores Journal or Contra entries.

==================================================
5. BANK BALANCE
==================================================

Apply exactly the same rule to bank ledgers.

A valid posted transaction affecting a bank ledger must update:

- Bank balance
- Cash & Bank screen
- Bank Book
- Dashboard Bank Balance
- financial reports
- Trial Balance

Payment, Receipt, Contra and Journal postings must all be reflected according to
their actual debit/credit entries.

Example Contra:

Dr Bank ₹50,000
Cr Cash ₹50,000

After posting:

Bank increases ₹50,000
Cash decreases ₹50,000

Dashboard, Cash Book, Bank Book and ledger balances must immediately agree.

The contra transaction must NOT change income, expense, payable or receivable unless
one of those ledgers is actually part of the transaction.

6. DO NOT CONFUSE CURRENT BALANCE WITH SETTLEMENT HISTORY
==================================================

This distinction is important.

Current outstanding balance and invoice settlement/allocation are NOT necessarily
the same thing.

The ERP may retain:

- individual invoice amount
- payment allocation
- invoice settlement status
- ageing information
- payment history

But the overall party CURRENT BALANCE must reconcile to the accounting ledger.

Do not silently mark a specific invoice as "paid by payment" if a journal merely
adjusted the vendor/customer control account and there is no explicit invoice
allocation.

If invoice-level allocation is needed, show it separately.

For example:

Vendor total ledger balance: ₹0
Specific purchase allocation: Unallocated adjustment / settled through journal

Do not fabricate payment allocations.

For ageing reports, determine and document how unallocated journal credits/debits
should reduce the party's total outstanding while preserving invoice-level history.
